High-temperature superconductivity in compressed ternary hydride compounds at 240 Kelvin

Mikhail Voronin, Liang Zhang, Elena Rostova et al.

Published 2026-03-21Physical Review LettersDOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.136.127001 ↗
Read original publication ↗
OPENSTEM EDITORIAL SUMMARY

What the research examines

Using diamond anvil cells coupled with synchrotron X-ray diffraction, physicists synthesize a clathrate ternary lanthanum-yttrium-hydride under 180 GPa pressure, observing a sharp superconducting resistance drop and magnetic susceptibility shielding at 240 Kelvin.

Why it may matter

Brings conventional electron-phonon superconductivity within reach of standard dry-ice temperatures.

What to examine carefully

Mega-bar pressure regimes necessitate microscopic diamond culet sample volumes, restricting wide industrial application.
