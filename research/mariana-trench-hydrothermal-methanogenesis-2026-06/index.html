Deep-sea hydrothermal vent ecosystems sustained by hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis in the Mariana Trench

Zhen-Hua Qian, Eleanor Brooks, Kenichi Watanabe et al.

Published 2026-06-16Nature GeoscienceDOI: 10.1038/s41561-026-01441-2 ↗
Read original publication ↗
OPENSTEM EDITORIAL SUMMARY

What the research examines

Autonomous deep-sea benthic landers sample hyper-alkaline serpentinizing hydrothermal vents at 10,900-meter depth, uncovering novel archaeal lineages that drive primary metabolic carbon fixation purely from mantle-derived molecular hydrogen and dissolved bicarbonate.

Why it may matter

Expands the environmental extremes of active biological metabolism with implications for exobiological life on icy ocean moons.

What to examine carefully

Extreme hydrostatic decompression during sample ascent poses risks of cell lysis and nucleic acid degradation.

Editorial scope: This is a concise, independently written introduction based on the publisher information, not a full systematic review or independent replication. It must not replace reading the original methods, limitations and conclusions.
