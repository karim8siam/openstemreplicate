How to develop a good STEM research idea today

Follow these steps independently, with a supervisor, or using an AI assistant while verifying its citations.

01

Choose a precise scientific problem

Start with a phenomenon, unsolved technical limitation, measurable variable or reproducibility concern.

02

Review foundational and recent work

Read highly relevant original papers. Keep a table of methods, datasets, results, weaknesses and citations.

03

Identify an evidence-based gap

Look for conflicting results, missing benchmarks, untested conditions, difficult assumptions or small experimental samples.

04

Write a testable question

Specify what you will measure or prove, the comparison, scope and expected uncertainty.

05

Design methods and baselines

Choose models, experimental controls, evaluation metrics, statistical tests and repeatability checks.

06

Check feasibility before committing

List required data, software, equipment, permissions, cost, timeline, safety and supervisor support.

07

Run a small pilot

Test one dataset, prototype or analytical case to discover problems before a major research commitment.

08

Document and revise

Record provenance, failures, evidence against the hypothesis and feedback from qualified reviewers.

A BETTER AI PROMPT

Start with evidence, not random topics.

Copy this template into your preferred AI assistant. Replace the bracketed fields and independently verify every source.

Act as a cautious STEM research mentor. I am a [academic level] student in [field], with skills in [skills], access to [equipment, data and software], and [available time]. My interest is [specific topic]. First ask for any essential missing constraints. Then help me identify 3–5 testable research questions informed by verifiable existing literature. For each, provide foundational papers and recent papers with real DOI links (mark any source you cannot verify), what earlier studies actually established, possible knowledge gaps (not guaranteed novelty), proposed hypotheses, methods and baselines, data and equipment needs, estimated complexity, risks, ethical requirements, and a low-cost pilot experiment. Challenge unrealistic ideas. Never invent citations or promise publication.
