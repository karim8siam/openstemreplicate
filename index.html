EDITOR'S SELECTION / SEPTEMBER 2026

Twenty papers. A month of discovery.

Selected for educational relevance, research interest, disciplinary diversity and traceable primary sources — not a global citation leaderboard. Our pilot issue draws exclusively from Nature Portfolio journals; future issues should diversify publishers.

Explore all twenty ↗
01Technology2026-09-16

Reimagining research papers as interactive and reliable AI agents

The researchers describe a system for turning scientific papers and associated resources into interactive AI agents. The work raises questions about how reliable executable paper assistants can be and how their outputs should be checked.

Nature·Jiacheng Miao et al.
Read analysis ↗
02Technology2026-09-07

Causal evidence that language models use confidence to drive behaviour

This study investigates how confidence-related signals affect language-model decisions about when to answer or abstain. It provides experimental evidence that changes to such signals can influence model behaviour.

Nature Machine Intelligence·Dharshan Kumaran et al.
Read analysis ↗
03Technology2026-09-10

A collaborative agent with two lightweight synergistic models for autonomous crystal materials research

The study presents an AI-agent arrangement using two smaller cooperating models for materials-research tasks. It examines a way to combine reasoning and scientific tool use without relying only on very large models.

Nature Machine Intelligence·Tongyu Shi et al.
Read analysis ↗
04Engineering2026-09-10

Autonomous robotic operation controlled by wave-based neuromorphic hardware

Researchers investigate controlling robotic operation using physical wave-based neuromorphic hardware. The work explores how unconventional computing devices might contribute to autonomous control.

Nature Communications·Jacob Zohar et al.
Read analysis ↗
05Science2026-09-25

Quantum field theory dynamics on a spin–phonon quantum computer

This paper explores quantum-field-theory dynamics using a hybrid computing platform with spins and phonons. The study illustrates the use of different quantum degrees of freedom in physical simulation.

Nature Physics·Anton T. Than et al.
Read analysis ↗
06Science2026-09-23

String-breaking dynamics in a quantum simulator

The authors investigate string-breaking phenomena using an engineered quantum simulator. It connects controlled laboratory dynamics with phenomena studied in lattice gauge theories.

Nature Physics·Arinjoy De et al.

OUR PUBLISHING POLICY

Deliberately selective, openly sourced.

We only publish the research we can substantiate. Each monthly edition is editorially reviewed before release.

How we select ↗
01

We begin with September 2026, then add each previous month's reviewed selection when ready.

02
